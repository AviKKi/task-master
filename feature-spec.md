# Task Master

## What it is

Task Master is a calm daily planning companion. It helps people turn an overwhelming list of work into small, clear actions, then rewards steady progress by growing a personal isometric garden or jungle.

The app is for people who want help deciding what to do next, not just a place to store a list.

## The main promise

Tell Task Master what needs to get done. Its AI planning companion helps turn it into steps that usually take about ten minutes each when that is practical. The person stays in control: they can accept, change, remove, or create any task themselves.

## First-time setup

The welcome flow should be brief, friendly, and explain why the app asks for each thing.

1. Welcome the person and introduce the idea of planning in small steps.
2. Ask for a name or preferred greeting, with an option to skip.
3. Explain that the person uses their own AI account, so they control which provider they use and pay that provider directly.
4. Let them choose OpenAI, Anthropic, or OpenRouter.
5. Let them paste their personal AI key. The key is hidden while typing, can be revealed on request, and can be changed or removed later in Settings.
6. Confirm the connection in simple language and begin with an empty plan or a suggested first task.

The person can skip AI setup and use the task list by itself. AI features should clearly invite them to finish setup when they choose to use them.

## Today

Today is the home screen and answers one question: "What should I do next?"

- Show the current date, a short encouraging greeting, and progress for the day.
- Put the next unfinished task first, with its estimated time when available.
- Show a short list of the remaining tasks planned for today.
- Let the person mark a task complete with one clear action.
- Offer an easy way to postpone a task, choose a different task, or ask the AI to make the task smaller.
- Celebrate completion gently without interrupting momentum.
- Give quick access to creating a task and starting a conversation with the AI.

## All Tasks

The All Tasks area is the person's complete, trustworthy record of work.

- Show tasks grouped in useful views such as Today, Upcoming, Later, and Completed.
- Let the person search for a task and filter by status, date, or project.
- Show parent tasks with their smaller steps so progress is easy to understand.
- Let the person open a task to see its notes, target date, estimated time, and smaller steps.
- Keep completed tasks available for review rather than making them disappear.

## Creating and editing tasks

People must be able to manage their work without the AI.

- Create a task from anywhere using a title and optional notes.
- Optionally add a target date, estimated time, project, and smaller steps.
- Edit every detail at any time.
- Reorder smaller steps and mark individual steps complete.
- Delete a task after a clear confirmation.
- Duplicate a task when a similar piece of work comes up again.
- Let a task remain a single item when it is already small enough; breaking work down is a suggestion, not a rule.

## AI planning companion

The AI companion is a chat-based planning partner, not an authority over the person's schedule.

- The person can describe work naturally, such as "I need to prepare for a presentation next Thursday."
- The companion asks a short follow-up only when it needs important missing information.
- It proposes a practical sequence of smaller tasks, aiming for about ten minutes each where possible.
- It may make larger or smaller steps when the work cannot reasonably fit that length, and should say why.
- Each proposed task shows a title, a short description if helpful, and an expected length of time.
- The person can add all suggestions, add selected suggestions, edit them before adding, or decline them.
- The companion can help reschedule a plan, clarify the next action, and turn a stalled task into a smaller starting point.
- It should be supportive and specific, never judgmental about missed tasks.

## Photos and voice in chat

The chat should accept more than typed messages.

- The person can choose or take a photo and send it with a question. For example, they might share a whiteboard, handwritten notes, or a page of instructions and ask for an action plan.
- The person can record and send a voice message, such as a quick brain dump while walking.
- Attachments appear in the conversation before sending so the person can remove them.
- The companion explains what it understood from an attachment and asks when something is unclear rather than guessing.
- The person can see which messages included a photo or voice recording.

## Garden and jungle reward

Completing tasks grows a personal isometric garden that gradually becomes a lush jungle. It is a visual record of showing up, not a scorecard for perfection.

- Every completed task contributes to growth.
- Finishing a small step still counts and should feel worthwhile.
- The garden begins simply, then gains paths, plants, trees, water, rocks, wildlife, and other details over time.
- Completing a group of related tasks can unlock a more noticeable garden addition.
- The garden never shrinks or punishes the person for taking time away.
- The person can visit the garden from the main navigation, pan around it, and see recent additions.
- A short note can explain which completed work helped create a new feature in the garden.

## Settings and control

- Let the person change their preferred AI provider or update their AI key.
- Let them remove an AI key and continue using manual task planning.
- Let them choose whether task completion celebrations are subtle or more noticeable.
- Provide a clear way to remove a task, chat, or attachment from the app.
- Explain in everyday language what information is shared with the chosen AI provider when the person sends a message.

## Tone

Task Master should feel focused, encouraging, and unhurried. It celebrates progress without using pressure, streaks, guilt, or shame. The garden should make small wins feel visible and cumulative.

## A successful first session

By the end of a first session, a person should be able to:

1. Add their own AI account or choose to continue without one.
2. Put one real piece of work into the app.
3. Turn that work into an approachable next action, with AI help if desired.
4. Complete or schedule a first small task.
5. Understand that completing tasks will grow their personal garden over time.
